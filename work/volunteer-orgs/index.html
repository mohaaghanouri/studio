Grant applications in three hours instead of two days, on free tools.

A registered Verein — around 40 active volunteers, one part-time paid coordinator, funded by a mix of small grants, membership fees and donations. Nobody there has a technology budget, and the coordinator is doing the work of three people on 20 hours a week. This engagement was partly unpaid and we would do it again.

The biggest problem is grant applications. Each funder wants the same information in a different structure — organisational description, project plan, budget, impact evidence, previous funding — and each application takes two full days. Those days come out of the delivery work, which is the whole point of the organisation. Behind that: rota coordination across 40 volunteers, membership admin, donor thank-yous and the annual report.

What we build

The core document
Every reusable fact about the organisation, written once, kept current, with a source for every number. The single most valuable thing we built, and it is essentially a well-organised text file.
The application drafter
You paste in a funder’s questions and word limits. Out comes a draft built from the core document. Two days becomes about three hours, and every claim traces back to something in the core — so nothing gets invented under deadline pressure, which is exactly when organisations invent things.
The deadlines diary
Application deadlines, reporting deadlines, and what was promised to whom. Missed reporting is how small organisations lose funders quietly.
The rota
Availability in, shifts out, reminders sent, gaps visible early. This was the coordinator’s largest weekly time sink, and it is pure scheduling.
The activity log
Thirty seconds after each session: what happened, how many people, anything notable. A year later the impact report is written from real records instead of a panicked reconstruction in January.

Where it stops

No impact claim is ever generated, and no message to a funder is sent automatically.

Every number in a grant application comes from the activity log or the accounts. If the log does not support it, the application says so. Overstating impact to a funder is fraud, it happens by accident under deadline pressure, and a fluent writing tool makes that accident easier — so the drafter is built to refuse. Where evidence is missing it writes "We do not yet measure this" and leaves the line for a person.

Nothing is written about a beneficiary. Where the organisation works with vulnerable people, no personal data enters any external tool at all. Aggregate counts only. And everything must run on free or near-free tools and be operable by whoever holds the role in two years — anything that needs a subscription or a specialist will be abandoned.

How you see it working

Hours per grant application. Applications submitted per year — which roughly doubled, and that is the entire economic point. Reporting deadlines met. Coordinator hours spent on admin versus delivery. Whether the activity log is still being filled in after six months.

When we say no

When an organisation wants a website, a CRM and a donor platform. That is a different project with an ongoing cost they cannot carry, and it will be abandoned within a year. A well-kept spreadsheet and a documented process outlive most systems built for organisations like this.

What it asks of you

Thirty seconds after each session to fill in the activity log. Keeping the core document current when something changes. Nothing that costs money every month.
