What we build

Meeting to action list
Recording or notes in, action list out: owner, deadline, dependency, open question, filed in your tracker. This closes the single biggest leak in project management: actions that are agreed in the room and lost before they reach the system.
The reminder
Owners get a message before their date, not after it. It is drafted in your own words and sent by you, so it stays a person asking a person. All three described chasing as the most draining part of the week, precisely because it is repetitive and slightly awkward.
The what-changed report
The status report is not a summary of the project. It is a summary of what changed. Software is good at spotting changes and people are bad at it. It lists: tasks moved, dates slipped, risks opened or closed, actions overdue. You write the two sentences that say what it means.
The knock-on list
When something slips, you get an immediate list of everything that depends on it. Doing this by hand in a large plan takes an hour, so it does not get done at the moment it matters.
The three audiences
One set of facts, three versions: steering committee, delivery team, client. Same facts in each. Nothing invented for any of them.

Where it stops

It does not set the traffic-light colour and it does not decide what to escalate.

The colour on a status report is a political and professional act. A tool that sets it either becomes a rubber stamp you override constantly, in which case it is noise, or it becomes something you defer to, which is worse.

It also never sends anything upward. Reports to a steering committee or a client are drafted by the tool and always leave under your signature.

How you see it working

Actions from meetings that land in the tracker: from about 60% to near all. Hours to produce the weekly report: typically four down to under one. Actions overdue without anyone noticing. Time from a slip to knowing what it affects downstream.

When we say no

When a programme is failing for reasons reporting cannot touch: no mandate, no resources, a sponsor who will not decide. Better reporting there only documents the failure more clearly. Occasionally people want exactly that, but they should want it knowingly.

What it asks of you

Recording the meetings you already attend, sending the reminders yourself, and writing two sentences of interpretation a week.

What clients say
