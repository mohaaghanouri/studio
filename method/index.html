How we decide what to build

Which part of your week repeats, and which part needs you?

Every person we have worked with arrived with a different job title and the same sentence: one part of my week has become unbearable. The profession changes. The shape of the problem almost never does.

We do not ask what you want automated, because almost nobody knows. You know what hurts. Our job is to find the exact line between the work that repeats and the work that needs your judgement, and to build only on the repetitive side of it. Finding that line is the hard part. Building the tool is the easy part.

How we tell one from the other.

Repetitive work — all four must be true

  1. It repeats. More than once a week, in roughly the same shape.
  2. It follows rules. If you had to explain how you do it, you could, in sentences rather than gestures.
  3. It is easy to check. A wrong result is obviously wrong to you within seconds.
  4. A caught mistake costs little. If the tool gets it wrong and you notice, you lose a minute, nothing more.

Your judgement — any one is enough

  1. You are liable for it. Your name, licence or signature is on it.
  2. Your taste is the product. People pay you precisely because the view is yours.
  3. The relationship is the product. A patient, a student, a client, a reader.
  4. It is a real decision under uncertainty, not a lookup with extra steps.

We build up to that line and never across it. Nearly every failed AI project we have been asked to rescue failed because someone built across the line, and the person quietly stopped trusting the results.

How it works

Four steps, every time.

  1. 01

    We watch your real week.

    Two to five days alongside your actual work. Not a workshop, not a questionnaire. The inbox, the folder names, the sticky notes, the workaround you are slightly embarrassed about. The workaround is usually where the tool belongs.

  2. 02

    We write down the line.

    One page that says what the tool will take over and what stays with you, read back to you. If something on the “take over” side makes you uneasy, you are right and we move it. That page is the contract.

  3. 03

    We build the smallest tool that does the job.

    Not a platform. One tool that takes the repetitive work off your desk. It is ready in weeks, not quarters, and it does one thing so completely that you stop thinking about it.

  4. 04

    We hand over the controls and leave.

    You can change the rules without us: the filters, the limits, the templates, the reasons something gets rejected. If a tool needs us to stay, we built it wrong.

When we say no.

We say no out loud, and we keep the relationship. Saying no is most of the reason people send us their friends and colleagues.

There is too little of it.
Under roughly an hour a week, keeping a tool running costs more than the task itself.
Every case is different.
If no two instances look alike, there is nothing repetitive to take over. There is only skill, and skill is not a bottleneck to remove.
One mistake would be too expensive.
If a single wrong result could end a career, the tool prepares the material and does nothing else.
A simple tool already exists.
A calendar, a form, a shared spreadsheet, a template. We set that up and bill for the afternoon, not the quarter.
It is a decision, not a workload.
Some people do not need a system. They need to decide something they have been avoiding. No software fixes that.

Two kinds of experience.

The company work teaches us what survives audits, handovers and colleagues who never asked for the tool. The one-to-one work keeps us honest about how little most people actually need.
