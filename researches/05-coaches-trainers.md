## Segment top 5

### 1. Conor Grennan — ai-mindset.ai (US)

The only site in this category that *proves* its product works instead of asserting it.
The homepage centres a Microsoft Research study: 388 Gap employees, 2.07× odds of
producing top-quality work, 77% hit-rate vs 62% baseline. Everything else — MasterClass
instructor, ex-NYU Stern Chief AI Architect, NASA/OpenAI/J.P. Morgan/McKinsey logo wall
— is ordinary category furniture, but the RCT reframes him from *speaker* to
*intervention with a measured effect*. Also runs a 3-question "Find Your Path" quiz and
a 2-minute Readiness Assessment as segmented lead capture rather than one generic form.

**Steal:** replace testimonial-stacking with one measured outcome study, even a small
self-run one.

### 2. Jarno Duursma — jarnoduursma.nl (NL)

The highest proof-density page found, and technically the cleanest: 78KB, no CMS
fingerprint, sub-second load. A stats bar sits directly under the hero (1,500+
presentations, 9.1 average rating, 71K social, 47K newsletter), followed by 18 named
enterprise clients (Unilever, BMW, PwC, ING, Nestlé, Rabobank) and testimonials that
carry **numeric event scores** — Michael Mackaaij rated him 9.4, Ruud Coumans 9.6.
Publishing an average rating *and* the individual scores that compose it is a trust
move almost nobody makes, because it only works if the numbers are good.

**Steal:** the numeric-rating testimonial format and the hero stats bar.

### 3. Zack Kass — zackkass.com (US)

Visually the best-executed site in the segment — a restrained Webflow build that trusts
photography and whitespace instead of gradient-mesh AI clichés, with a testimonial
carousel of 25+ entries anchored by the President of Coca-Cola and Morgan Stanley's
Chief Analytics & Data Officer. Sharp detail: an explicit disclaimer that he is *not*
affiliated with OpenAI, which pre-empts the obvious objection and reads as integrity
while still trading on the association. Substance is thin though — this is a keynote
business, not a training business.

**Steal:** the disclaimer-as-trust-device, and testimonial volume over testimonial length.

### 4. Dave Birss — davebirss.com (UK)

Aesthetically the weakest site in this top five (Elementor, Lato/Montserrat, 212KB), and
it still outperforms far prettier sites on conversion mechanics because it is the only
one showing **price and enrolment count together**: Prompt Advantage $197 / 470 students,
AI Unlocked $157 / 107 students, Creative Bootcamp $9.99 / 3,141 students. That single
formatting choice does testimonial work, price-anchoring work, and social-proof work at
once. Positioning is the other asset — "The Sensible AI Guy… actual intelligence on how
to use Artificial Intelligence" plus a "Snarky AI News RoundUp" stakes out anti-hype
ground in a category drowning in hype.

**Steal:** price + student count on the same card; a named enemy in the positioning.

### 5. The AI Exchange (Rachel Woods) — theaiexchange.com (US)

The cleanest offer architecture found: a single screen presents exactly two rungs —
$49.99/month membership labelled "LEARN THE ESSENTIALS / For Individuals Getting
Started" and a $3,000 "Certified AI Operator" labelled "LEAD THE CHARGE / For Operators,
Consultants & Team Leads Ready to Scale" — each with a who-it's-for line and a
three-bullet "you'll walk away ready to" outcome list. Naming the methodology (CRAFT
framework, "AI playbooks") turns a commodity workshop into proprietary IP, and the free
Playbook Template lead magnet is a genuine sample of the product rather than a PDF of
tips. 24KB Webflow build, near-instant. Badly let down by first-name-plus-initial
testimonials ("Dan C.", "Amanda L.") which read as fabricated.

**Steal:** two rungs only, each with an explicit "for whom" label and outcome bullets —
never more than two.
