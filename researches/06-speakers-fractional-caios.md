## Segment top 5

### 1. Mark van Rijmenam — thedigitalspeaker.com (EU/NL)

The only site here where copy, proof and conversion all land. The headline is an
actual argument — "AI is making every company smarter. It isn't making any of them
wiser" — followed by a subhead that names the buyer's real fear: "Leaders don't need
more information about emerging tech. They need better decisions they can defend."
Then he does the thing nobody else does and puts a *number on his own performance*:
NPS 9.2 across 200+ events in 32 countries. Steal the NPS-as-credibility move, the
WAVE framework diagram (Watch → Adapt → Verify → Empower), and the "Check
Availability" + 24-hour-response + downloadable brochure trio.

### 2. Mo Gawdat — mogawdat.com (US/UK)

The best hero architecture in the set. Two words, "HAPPINESS + AI", then the name,
then three labelled credentials stacked underneath — Leadership / Authorship / Mission
— each a category rather than a boast. It converts a CV into a three-second read. The
booking form is properly specified (inquiry type, date, event location, organization)
rather than a generic message box, and 50+ enterprise logos sit below the fold where
they belong. Custom-built, mission-led, no builder scaffolding — visibly more expensive
than everything around it.

### 3. Zack Kass — zackkass.com (US)

The most disciplined *visual* execution: Webflow, warm earth-toned palette, generous
space, one idea per screen. Positioning is three nouns done right — "Global AI Advisor
/ Speaker / Author" over "Empowering leaders to navigate the impacts and opportunities
of artificial intelligence." The inquiry form opens with a type dropdown (Keynote /
Media / Advisory / Other) so the lead self-triages before you read it, and commits to
a reply "within one business day." Docked a point on expertise for the unverifiable
"trusted by 80% of Fortune 500 CEOs" claim — though the footer disclaimer clarifying
he no longer speaks for OpenAI is a rare piece of honesty.

### 4. Kate O'Neill — koinsights.com (US)

Only site in 31 that opens with a question — "How can leaders make AI decisions that
truly serve humanity?" — which does what a statement hero can't: it makes the visitor
supply their own problem. The subhead then names the audience (Fortune 500 leaders),
the role ("the globally recognized 'Tech Humanist'") and the outcome in one sentence.
Speaking topics are split by buyer problem rather than by talk title, and the CTA is
"Check availability", which is what a booker types into an email anyway. WordPress,
but you would not guess.

### 5. Andrew Grill — actionablefuturist.com (UK)

Not the prettiest — the WordPress build is busy and the navy-and-teal palette is stock
corporate — but it wins on pure booking mechanics. "Enquire about fees & availability"
is the correct CTA text because fees and availability are literally the only two
questions a speaker buyer has, and almost every other site in this list hides both.
Press Kit, Testimonials and a live Calendar sit inside the Speaking submenu, so an
event producer can self-serve the whole pack. The four-stat bar (700 Keynotes | 50
Countries | 30 Years in Technology | 5× TEDx) is the cleanest credibility strip found.

## Cross-cutting findings

Fame and site quality are near-uncorrelated: Bernard Marr (3/10 design), Sascha Lobo
(4), Tom Goodwin (4) and David Shrier (1) are all vastly more famous than the
best-designed sites here.

The two habits that separate a working site from a brochure:

1. A hero that states a **thesis** or asks a **question** instead of listing job titles.
2. A CTA that names **fees, availability or a response time**.

Three genuinely rare moves worth copying:

- Paul Roetzer publishing his $60,000 floor
- Conor Grennan citing a Microsoft Research RCT instead of testimonials
- Jeremy Utley answering speaking logistics in a homepage FAQ

Squarespace and WordPress dominate. Zack Kass (Webflow) and Mo Gawdat (custom) are the
only two that read as designed rather than assembled.
