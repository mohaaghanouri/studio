# The Top 10

**242 site visits across 8 segments; roughly 200 unique sites after overlap.** Ranked on
the combined 40-point score (Design + Success + Quality + Expertise), then adjusted for
how much of the result is transferable to a solo practitioner rather than inherited from
fame.

Where two agents scored the same site, both numbers are shown and the average is used.

---

## 1. Mark van Rijmenam — thedigitalspeaker.com · Netherlands · 34/40

**Design 8 · Success 8 · Quality 9 · Expertise 9**

The only site in the whole sweep where copy, proof and conversion all land at once. The
headline is an actual argument rather than a job title: *"AI is making every company
smarter. It isn't making any of them wiser."* The subhead then names the buyer's real
fear — "Leaders don't need more information about emerging tech. They need better
decisions they can defend."

Then he does the thing nobody else does and puts a number on **his own performance**: NPS
9.2 across 200+ events in 32 countries. Not "clients love me" — a metric with a
denominator. The WAVE framework (Watch → Adapt → Verify → Empower) is drawn as a diagram
rather than asserted as a trademark, and the conversion trio is "Check Availability" +
a 24-hour response promise + a downloadable brochure.

**Why it's first:** it is the only site that is simultaneously well-designed, well-argued,
well-evidenced and easy to buy from. Everything above 30 points elsewhere is missing at
least one of those.

## 2. Mo Gawdat — mogawdat.com · US/UK · 34/40

**Design 8 · Success 10 · Quality 8 · Expertise 8**

The best hero architecture found anywhere. Two words — "HAPPINESS + AI" — then the name,
then three labelled credentials stacked underneath, each a *category* rather than a boast:

> **Leadership:** Former CBO, Google [X]
> **Authorship:** 4× bestselling author
> **Mission:** One Billion Happy

That converts a CV into a three-second read, and it is the single most portable pattern in
this document. The booking form is properly specified (inquiry type, date, event location,
organization) rather than a generic message box, and the 50+ enterprise logos sit below the
fold where they belong. Custom-built, no builder scaffolding — visibly more expensive than
everything around it.

**Caveat:** a large share of the Success score is inherited fame. The *hero structure* is
what transfers, not the résumé behind it.

## 3. Alice Labs — alicelabs.ai · Sweden · 33/40

**Design 7 · Success 9 · Quality 8 · Expertise 9**

The reference implementation for **proof**. Every case study is one line containing a named
client, a currency figure and a delivery window: Ljusgårda, $250K/year saved, 83% cost
reduction, shipped in six weeks. A public-sector client freeing 6,400–8,000 hours/year by
taking document handling from 60 hours to 3 minutes. Pricing is banded honestly rather than
hidden — focused pilot €25k–€60k, enterprise €80k–€250k.

And it is the only site in 200 that treats **European compliance as a selling feature**
rather than a legal footnote: PII redaction *before* the model call, prompt-injection
defenses, EU AI Act risk classification, audit logs, with Ragas and Langfuse named as the
eval stack. Five named humans on a 62 KB build.

**Directly relevant to you:** this is the EU-privacy argument done as marketing rather than
as an Impressum obligation.

## 4. Dr. Hubertus Porschen — hubertusporschen.com · Germany · 32/40

**Design 8 · Success 9 · Quality 8 · Expertise 7**

The best site in DACH, and the only German-market one that would survive comparison with
good US or UK work. Genuinely custom — asymmetric grid, real whitespace discipline, no
Elementor fingerprints. The proof is **layered rather than dumped**: enterprise logos
(Commerzbank, Allianz, Bosch, Siemens), then mid-market logos, then associations, then
press (ARD, FAZ, Handelsblatt), then a ProvenExpert score of 4.88 with 377 reviews behind
it.

Two things to take: the **dual-CTA footer** splitting "book a keynote" from "30-min
strategy call", so two very different buyer intents self-sort instead of fighting over one
button; and the anti-hype positioning line *"KI einbauen — wie eine gute Maschine"*, which
speaks the customer's vocabulary rather than the vendor's.

## 6. Kate O'Neill — koinsights.com · US · 32/40

**Design 7 · Success 9 · Quality 8 · Expertise 8**

The only site in 200 whose hero is a **question**: *"How can leaders make AI decisions that
truly serve humanity?"* This does something a statement hero structurally cannot — it makes
the visitor supply their own problem before you've made a claim.

The subhead then names the audience (Fortune 500 leaders), the role ("the globally
recognized 'Tech Humanist'") and the outcome in one sentence. Speaking topics are split by
**buyer problem** rather than by talk title, and the CTA is "Check availability" — which is
literally what a booker types into an email. WordPress underneath, and you would not guess.

## 7. Risto Uuk — ristouuk.com · Estonia/Belgium · 32/40

**Design 8 · Success 9 · Quality 6 · Expertise 9**

The best **typography** of any AI-category site, and it gets there by subtraction: Crimson
Text at a generous measure over warm paper (`#f9f8f5`), Inter for UI, one photograph, no
gradient, no glow, no pill badges. The entire hero is one paragraph of plain biography and
two buttons.

The authority is close to unmatchable — he wrote into the EU AI Act's general-purpose-AI
provisions, runs a 50,000-subscriber newsletter on it, sits on Estonia's PM-level AI
advisory board, Wiley book incoming. Ranked seventh rather than first because the Quality
score is honest: **there is no offer, no service, no price and no funnel.** It converts on
reputation alone, which is a luxury, not a strategy.

## 8. Zack Kass — zackkass.com · US · ~31/40

**Design 7–8 · Success 9 · Quality 7.5–8 · Expertise 6–7** *(three agents scored this
independently; range shown)*

The most disciplined **visual execution** in the AI category — a restrained Webflow build
with a warm earth-toned palette that trusts photography and whitespace instead of
gradient-mesh AI clichés, one idea per screen. Three devices worth taking:

- A **persistent bar above the nav** carrying the book title, a "BESTSELLER" chip, and the
  USA Today / LA Times marks. Press proof lands before any content does, for 40px of
  vertical space.
- Testimonials attributed **by role, not name** ("President, Coca-Cola"; "our highest-rated
  speaker ever", PayPal) — reads far more senior than a headshot carousel.
- An inquiry form that opens with a type dropdown (Keynote / Media / Advisory / Other), so
  the lead self-triages before you read it, plus a committed "within one business day."

Marked down for the unverifiable "trusted by 80% of Fortune 500 CEOs" and white hero type
over a busy stage photo. The footer disclaimer clarifying he no longer represents OpenAI is
a rare piece of honesty worth copying.

## 9. Conor Grennan (AI Mindset) — ai-mindset.ai · US · 31/40

**Design 5.5–7 · Success 8–8.5 · Quality 7–8 · Expertise 7–8**

Ranked ninth on a mediocre Squarespace build, entirely because of **one artifact**: the
homepage centres a Microsoft Research study of 388 Gap employees showing 2.07× odds of
producing top-quality work after a single session, 77% hit-rate versus 62% control.

Every other consultant in 200 sites is showing you where they have been. He is showing you
what measurably happens when someone uses him. That single reframe moves him from *speaker*
to *intervention with an effect size*, and it outranks any amount of art direction. He also
runs a 3-question "Find Your Path" quiz and a 2-minute Readiness Assessment as segmented
lead capture instead of one generic form.

**The lesson is the highest-leverage one in this entire document:** if you can manufacture
one defensible number — even from a small self-run study — it beats every logo wall here.

# What the whole sweep says

**1. Fame and site quality are almost uncorrelated.** Bernard Marr (design 3–4), Sascha
Lobo (4), Tom Goodwin (4) and David Shrier (1) are far more famous than anyone in the top
10. Sol Rashidi — world's first enterprise CAIO, 9 patents, Harvard Senior Fellow — has a
hero that is a dense grey paragraph with fifteen inline blue links.

**2. Design is a wide-open gap in this category.** In UK/IE, 14 of 24 sites run WordPress,
Squarespace, HubSpot or Divi/Avada with default type; only three had a deliberate font
pairing. In DACH it is worse. A genuinely crafted site is a cheap and durable edge here in
a way it would not be in, say, product design.

**3. Almost nobody publishes a number.** Across ~200 sites, roughly a dozen publish prices.
The ones that do (Roetzer's $60,000 floor, WorkWise's 25-item menu, Crux Digits' four-rung
ladder, Nicola Lazzari's three-currency day rates) stand out instantly — and in DACH,
where 20 of 24 hide behind "kostenloses Erstgespräch", a printed number is the cheapest
differentiation available.

**4. A logo you can't explain is decoration.** Parlance Labs and Prolego both show
extraordinary logo walls (OpenAI, Stripe, Netflix; Lockheed, Citi, Morgan Stanley) with
zero numbers or descriptions attached. One qualified sentence per logo would move either
site two points.

**5. Fake proof is worse than no proof.** Nicola Lazzari's site is beautifully built and
publishes real metrics — then attributes its testimonials to "Sarah Chen", "David Kim",
"Emma Rodriguez", no companies. A stock-name set retroactively makes a skeptical buyer
doubt the metrics too. **An empty testimonials section scores higher than a fabricated
one.** (Directly relevant: yours is currently empty by design. Keep it that way until real
quotes exist.)

**6. The single highest-leverage asset is one defensible number about your own effect.**
Not a logo, not a testimonial, not a framework — a measured outcome. Conor Grennan's
Microsoft RCT, Mark van Rijmenam's NPS 9.2, Jarno Duursma's per-event 9.4 and 9.6, Alice
Labs' "$250K/year saved in six weeks". Every one of those beats a wall of Fortune 500
badges.
