## Segment top 5

### 1. Justin McKelvey — justinmckelvey.com

The only site in the set where the design and the business model are solving the same
problem. The hero stacks availability-pill → italic serif display line → plain-language
subhead → a one-line metric strip, so you get positioning *and* proof in a single fold
without a logo wall. Behind it sits the clearest productized ladder anyone here has
published: a $2,500 two-week assessment as the entry drug, a $4,500 install, a $25–50K
rescue engagement, then open-ended fractional CTO. The weakness is that all four case
studies describe unnamed companies ("National Sports Platform"), so the 40% and 95%
figures are asserted rather than evidenced — but structurally this is the template to beat.

### 2. Vin Vashishta — vinvashishta.com

The only one that looks *designed* rather than assembled: a condensed grotesque display
face against a dark technical grid, mono type used as connective microcopy, one disciplined
cyan accent. More importantly it treats numbers as typography — the hero sets "$4B in
delivered value" in bold inside the subhead, then repeats 250K community / 9,000 trained /
34M impressions as mono metadata under it. That is proof used as visual rhythm, not a
stats bar bolted on at section three. It is also the clearest lesson in why craft needs QA:
the hero image is broken on load and the primary nav CTA is cyan on cyan, on a site selling
operational rigor.

### 3. Conor Grennan / AI Mindset — ai-mindset.ai

The site itself is a plain Squarespace build worth 5.5, and it ranks third anyway — which
is the point. It is the only consultant in this segment whose central claim is a controlled
study rather than a logo: Microsoft Research tested his framework on 388 Gap employees and
found 2.07× odds of top-quality output after one session, 77% vs 62% in control. Every
competitor is showing you where they've been; he's showing you what happens when you use
him. **If you can manufacture one defensible number like that, it outranks any amount of
art direction.**

### 4. Santiago Valdarrama — ml.school

The most efficient conversion page loaded. It opens with a five-star testimonial *above*
the H1 — borrowing credibility before making a claim — then states the offer in one
sentence, then puts "$500 / Next cohort: August 10–27, 2026" in a dark card in the first
screen. Price, scarcity and date are all resolved before you scroll, the opposite of the
"Contact Us for pricing" default across the rest of this table. Visually it's stock
Tailwind, but as an argument for one-offer pages it's the strongest evidence here.

### 5. Zack Kass — zackkass.com

Worth studying for two devices. First, the fixed bar above the nav carrying the book title,
a "BESTSELLER" chip, and the USA Today and LA Times marks — press proof lands before any
content does, and it costs 40px of vertical space. Second, testimonials attributed by role
rather than name ("President, Coca-Cola"; "our highest rated speaker ever" from PayPal),
which reads as more senior than a headshot-and-first-name carousel. The hero's white type
over a busy stage photo is the weak spot.

## Anti-pattern worth internalising

Parlance Labs and Prolego both display extraordinary logo walls (OpenAI, Stripe, Netflix;
Lockheed, Citi, Morgan Stanley) and then attach *zero* numbers, testimonials or
descriptions to any of them. **A logo you can't explain is decoration.** One qualified
sentence per logo would move either site up two points on Success.
