## On the template-cloning problem

It's real and it has a price tag. Omakase's own catalogue explains why you keep seeing the
identical ROI calculator, identical "01/02/03" process strip, and identical
industry-tagged case grid across unrelated agencies — that's KYMA ($129) and Operator ($99).

**Framer itself is not the tell; Ace Studio scores a 9 on Framer. The tell is Framer *plus
default type*, and it's visible in seconds.** The bigger DACH-specific problem is
Elementor: n8n-agentur.de and press-ki.de both ship 220–250 KB of page-builder soup, and
it reads "cheap" before a visitor has read a word.

## Segment top 5

### 1. Alice Labs — alicelabs.ai (Stockholm)

The reference implementation for proof. Every case study is a named client, a currency
figure, and a delivery window in one line: Ljusgårda at $250K/year saved, 83% cost
reduction, shipped in six weeks; a public-sector client freeing 6,400–8,000 hours/year by
taking document handling from 60 hours to 3 minutes. Pricing is banded honestly rather
than hidden — focused pilot €25k–€60k, enterprise €80k–€250k — which qualifies buyers
without pretending a fixed price exists.

And it is the only site in the set that treats European compliance as a *selling feature*
rather than a footnote: PII redaction *before* the model call, prompt-injection defenses,
EU AI Act risk classification, audit logs, Ragas and Langfuse named as the eval stack.
Five named humans including two co-founders, on a 62 KB Webflow build.

### 2. Crux Digits — cruxdigits.nl (Netherlands)

The most complete boutique package outside Sweden. It has all four things almost nobody
has simultaneously: named enterprise clients (Guidewire, Carrier, Stad Antwerpen),
percentage-level ROI (+8% revenue, +7% capacity; ~100,000 assessments processed), a
four-rung published ladder (€150/hr → €2,500 audit → €20,000 PoC → from €50,000
production, ex-VAT), and a stack that proves real ML work rather than Zapier-with-a-hat
(PyTorch, LangChain, Mistral, Docker, Postgres). The Astro build with Space Mono as a
display face reads distinctive rather than templated.

Steal the differentiator line in spirit: *"By the second call, you get a working MVP — not
a 60-page document."*

### 3. Ace Studio — acedesign.io

The design ceiling for this category, and proof that the Framer complaint is about
templates, not about Framer — this is `Framer 65a4904` with custom Anton, Chivo Mono and
Geist Mono `@font-face` declarations, and it looks nothing like the $129 clones. More
importantly it demonstrates that in a design-literate audience, one testimonial from
shadcn at Vercel outweighs any ROI table: social proof should be chosen for who your buyer
already trusts, not for the size of the logo. The posture is right for a 1–5 person shop —
"We go deep with a few" beside a live capacity line.

The execution slip is instructive: the scarcity copy reads "TAKING 1 NEW PROJECTS FOR
JULY" — a typo, and stale by a month. **Scarcity copy is a maintenance commitment; stale
scarcity is worse than none.**

### 4. WorkWise Solutions — workwisesolutions.org (US)

The single most stealable pricing page in the category, and simultaneously the clearest
warning about pricing without proof. Roughly 25 offers each carry one published number,
from a $5,000 screening report to a $250,000 AI Operating System, with monthly retainers
at $10k/$15k/$20k on a 3-month minimum and annual prepay earning a free month.

The mechanics are what to copy: the $7,500 Executive Briefing credits 100% toward any
program booked within 90 days, size brackets apply only to rollouts and retainers, and the
framing does the closing — *"Buying AI consulting usually means three calls before anyone
says a number. We think that wastes your time."* The six-verb IA (Assess / Enable / Build /
Sustain / Govern / Research) is a better nav than anyone else in this set. Then it
collapses: not one client, case study, or testimonial anywhere.

### 5. Nicola Lazzari — nicolalazzari.ai (UK/Italy)

The best-executed genuinely-solo site here and the sharpest cautionary tale. The design is
real work, not a purchase — Next.js with Schibsted Grotesk and Spline Sans Mono, claiming
100/100/100/100 PageSpeed. It's the only site publishing day rates split by service line
*and* in three currencies (AI Consultant £700/€820/$890, AI Apps £820/day, Frontend
£440/day), which removes discovery-call gatekeeping without committing to fixed scope.
Case studies are formatted as three hard metrics each — 18% trial-to-paid lift, 1.9s first
token / 92% grounded answers / 2.4× CTA conversion — the correct unit of proof.

But the testimonials are "Sarah Chen", "Emma Rodriguez", "David Kim", "Lisa Anderson",
each with a job title and no company: a stock-name set that reads as fabricated and
retroactively makes a skeptical buyer question the metrics too. **An empty testimonial
section would score higher than that one.**
